Privacy Policy

Last updated: October 29, 2026

Meet Me Halfway has no server of its own. It runs in your browser and uses the third-party services listed below. This page describes what leaves your device, who can see it, and how long it is kept. “We” means whoever runs this copy of the app: the source code is open, and anyone can host it.

In short

  • While you are in a session, your precise location is shared with everyone who has the session link, whether or not they joined.
  • Your location is deleted when you stop sharing: when you close the page, when your connection drops, or when you turn off location permission.
  • A small session record — the code, when it was created, and random IDs for the people in it — is not deleted automatically. After 24 hours nobody but us can open it.
  • Mapbox receives everyone's location to draw the map and the routes. If venue search is on, Google receives the midpoint.
  • If error reporting is on, locations and session codes are removed from reports before they leave your device.

What you share with a session

While a session is open on your device and location permission is on, the app publishes the following and updates it every few seconds:

  • your latitude and longitude, as precise as your device reports them, with their accuracy and the time of the update;
  • a display name: the name you typed into the session badge, or otherwise your device type, such as “iPhone” or “Windows”;
  • a random ID created by Firebase Anonymous Authentication. It is not linked to your name, email, phone number or any account.

In the database, each update replaces the one before it, so no history of where you have been is kept there.

Who can see it

  • Anyone who has the session link. The 6-character code in the link is the only key. Anyone who has it can see the live location, display name and random ID of everyone sharing in that session — without joining, and even after the session is full. Up to 5 people can join. Share the link only with people you want to see your location, and remember that they can pass it on.
  • Nobody but us, after 24 hours. From 24 hours after a session was created, the database refuses to open it to anyone.
  • We can see everything stored in the database, at any time, through the Firebase console. That includes sessions older than 24 hours.

How long it is kept

  • Your location and display name are kept until you stop sharing. Closing the page or turning off location permission deletes them. If the page closes abruptly or your connection drops, the database deletes them as soon as it notices the connection has gone. If your connection comes back while the page is still open, the app starts sharing again.
  • The session record holds the session code, when the session was created, and the random IDs of the person who created it and of everyone who joined. It is not deleted automatically. It stays in the database until we delete it; after 24 hours, nobody but us can open it.
  • Your random ID is kept by Firebase Authentication, with when it was created and last used, until we delete it. Your browser keeps it too, so a later session reuses it. If your browser blocks storage, you get a new ID each time.
  • Error reports are kept by Sentry for the retention period of our Sentry plan.
  • Mapbox and Google keep what they receive according to their own policies, linked below.

Services that receive data

  • Firebase (Google Cloud) stores the session data described above in the Firebase Realtime Database, and issues your random ID through Firebase Authentication. See Google's privacy policy.
  • Mapbox draws the map and works out routes. See Mapbox's privacy policy.
    • The map is downloaded for the area on screen, which is around the people in the session, so Mapbox receives your IP address and the area you are looking at.
    • To show everyone's route, your device asks Mapbox for a route from each person in the session to the meeting point — the midpoint, or the venue you picked. Mapbox therefore receives every participant's precise location, from every participant's device.
    • Mapbox's map software keeps a random identifier in your browser, replaces it every 24 hours, and sends it to Mapbox each time a map loads. Mapbox uses it for billing.
  • Google Places (if venue search is on) receives the midpoint and a search radius, to find places nearby. See Google's privacy policy.
  • Google reCAPTCHA (through Firebase App Check) checks that requests come from this app and not from a bot. It runs in the page, sends information about your device and browser and how you use the page to Google, and may set a cookie for that purpose. This site is protected by reCAPTCHA, and the Google Privacy Policy and Terms of Service apply.
  • Sentry (if error reporting is on) receives reports of errors, and of how long pages and requests take on about 1 in 10 visits. Before a report leaves your device, any locations, map areas and session codes are removed from it. What remains:
    • the error itself;
    • your browser and operating system versions;
    • the page address, and the app's recent activity, such as the buttons you tapped, the requests it made and its log messages;
    • the first two characters of the session code;
    • whether you were online, and whether you were in an in-app browser.
    If your device cannot provide a location, the report says which error the browser gave and whether location permission was granted. Sentry is told not to record your IP address. See Sentry's privacy policy.
  • WhatsApp, Waze and Google Maps receive data only when you tap their buttons. Sharing on WhatsApp sends WhatsApp the session link. The navigation buttons hand the meeting point to Waze or Google Maps.

What stays on your device

  • Your Firebase sign-in, so a later session reuses your random ID.
  • Your display name and language choice.
  • Mapbox's random identifier, and the map pieces its software saves so maps load faster.
  • The app's own files, so it can open without a connection.

Clearing this site's data in your browser removes all of it.

Your choices

  • Stop sharing by closing the page, or by turning off location permission in your browser. Either way, your location and display name are deleted from the database at once. To share again, turn permission back on and tap Try again, or reload the page.
  • Choose the name others see by tapping your name in the session badge.
  • Share the link carefully. For 24 hours, it opens the session to anyone who has it.
  • Clear this site's data in your browser to remove what is stored on your device.
  • Ask us to delete a session record through the contact below.

Children

Meet Me Halfway is not directed at children under 13. We do not knowingly collect data from children.

Changes to this policy

We will update the “Last updated” date above when this policy changes. Material changes will be announced on the project's GitHub repository.

Contact

Open an issue on the GitHub repository for privacy questions or deletion requests. Issues are public, so do not post the code of a session that is less than 24 hours old: until then, the code opens the session.
